Would you like to share your perspective about gravity with the next generation of graduate students?

The São Paulo School on Gravitational Physics (GraSP) is now heading to its fourth edition. It continuees to be an initiative led by early-career physicists striving to explore and promote gravity, in all of its aspects. We are now searching for new and old friends and collaborators who are interested in joining this effort in 2027. Namely, we are looking for lecturers who would like to teach an 8-hour minicourse on a topic of their choice.

As GraSP's name suggests, we are looking for courses related to gravity. Luckily, this is a very far-reaching subject, and several topics can fit this description. In previous editions, we have had courses on classical general relativity, astrophysics, cosmology, quantum field theory in curved spacetimes, theoretical aspects of high-energy physics with a view toward gravity, special relativity, philosophy of relativity, among other topics. We gravitate toward any topic in which something is falling, or in which spacetime plays a key role. Gravity does not need to be relativistic, and other topics in physics that can be motivated by gravity are welcome as well. You are encouraged to be creative and suggest your ideas!

There is, however, an important constraint: our target audience is comprised of advanced undergraduate students and early graduate students. While this is not a restriction on the topics that can be covered, it is a restriction on the approach. We want courses that can guide young students through their future choices during graduate school. The courses should teach them a bit about how research in gravity has been going, but it should also take into account that our typical students have recently entered graduate school (or are about to). These courses are typically offered in English.

If you are willing for an extra challenge, we also welcome "introductory courses" for students who have not yet taken a course in general relativity. Past examples include gravitational wave astrophysics, black hole physics, special relativity, and compact objects. The key constraint is that these courses must not assume previous experience with general relativity, and ideally should assume as few prerequisites as possible. We often try to offer these courses in Portuguese, but submissions in English are welcome as well.

We value courses that encourage a hands-on component, encouraging the students to participate in the lectures actively. Each course in the past has handled this in different ways: live activities, coding challenges, solving exercises with the students, Q&A sessions, and so on. Feel free to think what would fit your course best and pursue that route.

Our ideal lecturers are young researchers. We want GraSP to be a stage where graduate students and postdocs can gain traching experience, and in exchange assist the next generation. There have also been past courses taught by junior faculty, but we prefer to focus on researchers that do not yet have a permanent position. We particularly encourage applications by members of underrepresented groups in academia.

In addition to the lectures themselves, we also organize a few panel sessions with lecturers and other invited researchers at the end of the school. We would appreciate if all lecturers could join these discussions with the students.

Last, but not least, we want the impact of the school to last. For this reason, we always prefer to record the lectures and make them available on YouTube for other students to see. You are encouraged to visit our YouTube channel to check a few of our old recordings and see how they happened. In addition, we encourage lecturers to arXiv and publish their lecture notes, so they can join the standard scientific record and benefit both the community, and the lecturers themselves.

We are planning IV GraSP to occur in July 2027, although the precise dates are still pending. If you would like to propose a minicourse, please fill the form below. You can polish the title and abstract of the course a posteriori. For now, we would only like to have a broad idea of the topics and approach. We do not impose a strict deadline (i.e., we are willing to increase the number of courses in the school, or to consider the course to a future edition), but we would prefer to receive submissions still in 2026.

We are eagerly waiting for you!
